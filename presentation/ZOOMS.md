- The knob is a plain, high-contrast ring, readable on any colour.
- The target is a dotted outline, so it stays visible while it fills.
- The coaching is always a direction ("sharper", "softer"), never "wrong".

## How we used AI

> "AI served as an accelerator for research and for exploring different directions, but human judgement was always needed."

**It showed us many directions, and we judged which ones actually worked in practice.**

Show these, ideally side by side:
- `process/lights-star.jpg`: early "sound star" directions, a cloud of particles that gathers into a star.
- `process/orb-states.jpg`: the particle globe folding onto the star as the sound gets accurate.
- `explorations/sparkbook/` (run it, or screen-record it): the first draft, where the voice draws particles that turn into the character.
- `process/styles.jpg`: different scene-reaction styles for SSS and SHH.
- `process/dark.jpg` vs `process/light1.jpg`: dark theme vs light theme.
- `process/glass-phones.jpg` and `process/ring.jpg`: slider and knob variations, glass vs plain ring.

> "Claude generated a lot of options quickly: particle stars, glowing orbs, glass sliders, dark and light themes. We
> looked at each one critically and asked whether it would actually work for a five-year-old with a cochlear implant.
> The particle star was beautiful, but children couldn't tell which way to change their sound. So we moved to a simple
> slider between the two sounds. The glass knob looked nice but was hard to see, so we made it a plain, high-contrast ring.
> Dark mode looked polished, but a bright storybook felt friendlier for young children."

**We added our own ideas on top and raised the quality bar.**

Show: the initial illustration vs the brushed-up one.
- Before: the original vector snake, in `process/styles.jpg` or `soundbook/original-v2.html`.
- After: `zooms/01-characters.png` (Sid and Squirrel, redrawn).

> "We didn't just take the suggestions as they were. We added our own ideas, like one-direction pages, a dotted target
> zone, and a silhouette of who's about to appear, and we redrew the characters ourselves. AI gave us breadth; our
> judgement gave us the right answer, and pushed the quality higher."

**Closing line for this section:**
> "AI made exploring cheap. Deciding what was right for these children stayed our job."
